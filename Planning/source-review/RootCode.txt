System Requirements Specification (FR & NFR)

Sep 29, 2026 · @Sanjeevan

Introduction

This specification merges the four separately-written role documents (Store Manager, Dispatcher,
Loader, Driver) for Waypoint, the delivery-coordination system built for Tech-Triathlon 2026, into one
consistent functional and non-functional requirements baseline.

Waypoint coordinates deliveries from two depots (Peliyagoda, Kandy) to 120 outlets across three
brands with very different rhythms: Fresh (80 outlets, near-daily chilled + dry goods, must arrive
before 8 AM), Style (25 outlets, weekly, half inside mall access windows), and Tech (15 outlets,
as-needed, high-value/fragile single items). A fleet of 60 vehicles runs up to two trips a day.

The challenge booklet names four problems the system must fix: planning is fragmented (orders
arrive by phone or message and are re-entered by hand); deferrals lack a clear record (the same
outlet can go unserved on consecutive runs, unnoticed); communication doesn't support feedback
(no reliable proof of delivery or issue record); and demand is difficult to anticipate. Every
requirement below traces back to one of these problems, to an explicit operating constraint, or is
flagged as a team-proposed addition that closes a gap the brief implies but never states.

Four roles carry the workflow from order to resolution: the Store Manager places orders and
confirms receipt, the Dispatcher turns the order queue into a feasible, constraint-respecting delivery
plan, the Loader loads vehicles against that plan and flags shortfalls before departure, and the Driver
executes the trip and records what actually happened. This document first sets out the requirements
that cut across all four roles, then documents each role in turn, and closes with the additional
features and shared data model that tie the roles together.

The design principle carried through every screen is Drive → Stop → Interact → Drive:
safety-relevant interaction only happens once the vehicle is stopped. The one required degradation
scenario is connectivity loss during an active trip — the Driver keeps working from cached trip data,
records outcomes locally, and syncs automatically on reconnection, with failed records left retryable
rather than lost.

Shared Data Entities

All four roles read and write the same underlying records: Outlet (brand, district, depot, dock type,
mall/delivery window), Vehicle (type, temperature capability, weight/volume limits, fuel quota,
depot), Order/Delivery (outlet, brand, type, temperature requirement, units/weight/volume, status
history), Trip/Route (vehicle, stop sequence, planned times), Delivery Outcome and Proof of
Delivery (photo, signature, receiver name, timestamp), Issue Case (lifecycle, attributed stage,
evidence, outcome), and Sync Record (for offline reconciliation). Keeping one definition of each
entity, referenced identically by every role's screens, is what makes the four separate documents
behave as a single consistent system rather than four separate apps that happen to share a name.

End-to-End Flow
Store places an order → Dispatcher allocates it to a vehicle and trip and publishes the plan (or defers
it, with a reason, back to the Store) → Loader loads the vehicle against the published sequence and
flags any shortfall → Driver executes the trip and records the delivery outcome with proof → Store
confirms receipt or reports an issue → any issue is resolved through the fair-attribution process,
feeding outlet service-health metrics and driver scoring back into the Dispatcher's view. This loop,
and the requirement that every step in it is visible to the roles it affects, is the throughline connecting
all four documents.
